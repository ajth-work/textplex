# Audience Segments

TextPlex should segment users by learning intent, behavior, and declared goals rather than by sensitive identity assumptions.

## Segment model

| Segment | What it means | Useful signals | Messaging angle | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Curious explorer | A user who is evaluating whether the product fits their reading or language goals | First visit, shallow browsing, sample interaction | Show value quickly, with a low-friction entry point | Overexplaining, hard sell language |
| Goal-driven learner | A user who wants measurable progress toward a target | Goal selection, repeated sessions, completion of setup | Emphasize structure, milestones, and progress visibility | Vague promises or generic feature lists |
| Habit builder | A user trying to read consistently | Repeat visits, regular short sessions, notifications enabled | Emphasize continuity, small wins, and steady momentum | Guilt-based nudges or streak pressure |
| Returning learner | A user coming back after a pause | Inactivity followed by a return visit | Offer a simple re-entry path and remind them where they left off | Shame language or penalty framing |
| Support-seeking learner | A user who wants more scaffolding or explanation | Higher help usage, repeated lookup, settings adjustments | Emphasize clarity, control, and guided support | Talking down to the learner |
| Deep reader | A user who wants autonomy and immersion | Longer sessions, page progression, lower help dependency | Emphasize control, depth, and minimal interruption | Over-notifying or cluttering the reading flow |

## Recommended profile fields

Collect the smallest useful set of first-party fields:

- Primary goal
- Reading comfort level
- Preferred content type
- Session frequency
- Support level desired
- Languages involved
- Notification preference

## Practical rule

If a segment can only be identified by guessing at a sensitive trait, do not use that segment. Use declared goals or observed product behavior instead.
